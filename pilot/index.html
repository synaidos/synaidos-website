How a pilot runs

Agree it, walk it, score it, decide.

The order matters. Success criteria are fixed before any scan, and our answers are fixed before your count, so nobody grades their own homework.

  1. 1

    Agree

    Which fixtures, which stores, what counts as a unit, where product identity comes from, and the numbers that mean success. Privacy and retention are agreed here too.

  2. 2

    Walk

    A short guided phone pass per bay, on a visit someone already makes. The app turns down a pass that can't support a measurement.

  3. 3

    Score

    We freeze our results, then your team counts. Every position is scored, including the ones we marked unknown.

  4. 4

    Decide

    A written report against the agreed criteria, and a go/no-go on the next step. A pass on one bay is a reason to test a store, not a general claim.

Scope

Narrow on purpose.

A first pilot proves one thing well. Anything outside this list is a later conversation, once the measurement has earned it.

In a first pilot

  • Retail product shelving: gondola shelves, cooler doors and lane systems
  • Lane-by-depth status: stocked, gap or unknown
  • Count ranges per lane, rolled up to products with a confirmed lane map
  • The fixture as built: lane spacing, depth and shelf extent
  • An evidence record behind every result

Not in a first pilot

  • Every fixture type in the store at once
  • Continuous, real-time monitoring
  • Automatic replenishment or ordering
  • Stockroom and back-of-house reconciliation
  • Permanent hardware in the store
Questions

Before you book.

The short answers. The walkthrough call covers the rest.

Who walks the bays?

For the bay check, we do. From the store pilot onward, the aim is for your own staff or merchandisers to scan on visits they already make, because that is what a rollout would look like.

Do we need to install anything?

No. A pilot needs a phone and store access. There are no shelf sensors, fixed cameras or network changes.

What happens to the footage?

Retention, who can see footage and where it is stored are written into the pilot agreement before the first scan. Results and reports are yours.

What if the system isn't sure?

It says so. Positions it can't support are marked unknown and scored as part of the result, not dropped. Passes too poor to measure are rejected at capture.

What do you share, and what do you keep?

The results are yours: the scored report, the unknowns and the evidence behind each call, to share with whoever you like. What we keep proprietary is the method: how the capture is guided and how the measurement is made.

How do you know the numbers are honest?

Our results are frozen before your hand count exists, then scored against it. You hold the truth until we've committed to an answer.
